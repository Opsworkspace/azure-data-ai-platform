# 0008. Set the availability SLO at 99.95%

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Context

Every expensive decision in this platform — the second region, zone
redundancy, AKS Standard tier, Cosmos multi-region writes, `maxUnavailable: 0`
— is justified by an availability target. Without a stated number, none of
them can be justified or challenged.

The instinct is to pick a number that sounds impressive. That is how platforms
end up committed to targets their architecture cannot deliver, which teaches
everyone that the SLO is decorative.

## Decision

**99.95%** availability for the API, measured as the proportion of HTTP
requests not returning 5xx, over a rolling 30-day window.

Error budget: **21.6 minutes per month.**

A corresponding external SLA, were one offered, would be 99.9% — deliberately
looser, so the objective can be missed without a contractual consequence.

The AI assistant carries a separate, lower objective of 99.9%, and its
dependency is marked non-critical in the readiness check so that its failure
degrades one feature rather than removing a region from rotation.

## Consequences

### What this makes easier

- Every architectural cost has a justification that can be argued with.
- Alerting has a basis: `modules/observability/alerts.tf` computes burn-rate
  thresholds directly from this number, so the dashboards and the Terraform
  cannot disagree.
- The error budget converts "should we prioritise reliability?" from an opinion
  into a measurement. Budget spent by the 10th means feature work pauses.

### What this makes harder

- 21.6 minutes per month is not much. A single bad deployment can consume a
  significant fraction, which is why `maxUnavailable: 0`, PodDisruptionBudgets
  and progressive rollout are not optional here.
- It commits the platform to dual-region operation permanently. That is the
  single largest ongoing cost.

### What would have to change for this to be wrong

If the product turned out to tolerate more downtime than assumed — an internal
tool, a batch-oriented workflow — 99.9% single-region would cut cost by roughly
half. The number should be revisited against real usage once the product is
live, not defended out of sunk cost.

Conversely, if a customer contract demanded 99.99%, the honest answer is that
this architecture cannot deliver it (see below) and the design would need to
change, not the number.

## Why not 99.99%

Because the architecture cannot deliver it.

Composing the hard dependencies' published SLAs, and accounting for the fact
that dual-region removes the per-region AKS control plane from the product:

```
Front Door        0.9999
AKS (dual-region) 0.99999975
Cosmos DB         0.99999
Entra ID          0.9999
Networking        0.9999
                  ─────────
                  0.99969   →  99.97% theoretical ceiling
```

99.95% leaves roughly 0.02% of headroom for our own bugs, our own deployments
and our own mistakes — which the arithmetic cannot model and which dominate in
practice.

Committing to 99.99% would mean committing to a target above the platform's
theoretical ceiling, before the application has failed once. The full working
is in [`docs/architecture/02-availability-model.md`](../architecture/02-availability-model.md).

## Alternatives considered

**99.9%.** 43.2 minutes per month. Achievable single-region with zone
redundancy, and roughly half the cost. Rejected because a consumer-facing
product with 1,000,000 users has no tolerance for a regional outage lasting
hours, and Azure regional incidents of that length do occur.

**99.99%.** 4.32 minutes per month. Above the theoretical ceiling computed
above. Rejected as dishonest.

**No SLO.** The default for many platforms. Rejected because it makes every
cost decision unarguable in both directions — nothing can be justified, and
nothing can be cut.
